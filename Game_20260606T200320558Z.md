The blue has no bottom. No surface. No horizon. You hang in it and there is nothing to push against, no direction that is more true than another. You are aware that you exist but the shape of you hasn't decided what it is yet. The only thing that has form is the thing you are holding onto without knowing you are holding onto it — a single concept, a single truth, the thing you will become.

The blue moves around you. Not waves. Not currents. Just the endless same-blue, pressing in from every side, patient and total. You are not alone in this place. Every drifter is alone here — even when two are technically nearby they cannot find each other, cannot hear each other, cannot reach. The Ocean does not connect. It only holds.

There is nothing to do but wait. The question is whether you will wait as nothing, or whether the thing you are holding will take weight and become the thing that lifts you out.

You hear something. A voice, maybe. Or the memory of a voice. It is calling from above and it sounds like your idea sounds, like the thing you are clinging to. It says come up, darling.

The blue thins above you. Something is waiting on the other side.

What do you do?

## Turn 1

**Player:** I cling to the understanding that you cannot take what is not offered. The blue holds everything — but I am not here to be held. I move toward the thinning. I have the weight and I know what it costs.

**Game Master:**

The blue has a texture you did not notice before. Pressing back is not the same as not pressing at all. The thing you are carrying has edges now — it is not yet a shape, but it is the shape of a push, and you are using it. You move through the blue the way a hand moves through water toward something held just above the surface: clumsy, determined, aware that the reaching itself is a kind of proof.

The voice above is still there. Closer now. The thinning it promised is not a hole — the blue simply becomes less itself the higher you go, until what holds you becomes what you are, and that is the moment of becoming. You are not there yet. You are in the part where the blue notices you noticing it.

Someone is hanging above you in the same blue, arms spread, suspended in the waiting. They are not moving toward the surface. They are waiting for something to move through them first — the way a coin waits in a fountain, patient and tarnished and certain that someone will eventually reach. They turn their head slightly and you cannot tell if they see you.

The blue between you is thin enough to hear through. Not words. A feeling. The feeling of someone who has been here long enough to know what the surface sounds like when it breaks.

The thinning is above. The weight is yours. The cost is already paid — you paid it the moment you decided not to be held.
