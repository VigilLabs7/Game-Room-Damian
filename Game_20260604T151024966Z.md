Blue, in every direction. The same blue you have always known — or the only blue you know, which amounts to the same thing. You are suspended in it, weightless, formless. There is no surface to reach for, no floor beneath you, no sound but the silence of your own awareness. Somewhere, faintly, a single truth is taking shape inside you — a thing you would die for, or live for, though you do not yet have the words for either.

You are not alone. You know this the way you know your own name — dimly, like something recalled from a great distance. Others have been here. Others have risen out of this same blue. You will not meet them here. The Ocean keeps its own.

Something is about to change.

Show me who you are.

**Game Master:**

The words come first — not yours, not a name, but something older. *Not them.* Two syllables pressed into the silence like a seal into wax. Twelve tags in your pocket. You don't remember putting them there. You don't remember the pockets. But your hand finds them anyway, cool weight against your hip, and the last one has a word on it.

You are in the blue. You have always been in the blue. Somewhere behind you — or above you, or before you — a square is trying to form. Cobblestones. A fountain. A figure turning. The blue is releasing you and you are not yet sure you want to go.

You know things you should not know. The name H. Voss sits in your memory like a stone in a shoe — there, wrong, impossible to ignore. The transport schedule. The men on it. The way the empire's machinery moves people like cargo and calls it necessity. You know this the way you know your own heartbeat: without being told, without choosing to remember, as if someone else lived this knowledge into you before you were you.

Your hand tightens around the tag. The word is the only thing that is yours.

Someone beside you is deciding to speak. The blue thins around the edges of that sentence — the figure by the fountain, the beginning of a question, the moment before the world says *here is where you are and here is what you must do.*

You are not holding on yet. But you are aware of the edge.

## Turn 2

**Player:** I turn toward the figure before they finish deciding. The word on the tag is mine — I don't know yet what it means, but I know it's not theirs. I pull the tag from my pocket and hold it between two fingers, not offering it, just letting it be seen. "You know H. Voss," I say. It's not a question. "Who wanted them stopped?"

**Game Master:**

The question lands before he's ready. You see it in the way he sits — the shoulders drawing back half a beat too late, the eyes narrowing not in suspicion but in the particular blankness of someone reaching for something that isn't there.

"Harbor Coordinator Voss," he says. He weighs the name as he says it, which means he's heard it before, somewhere, the way you hear a street name you've walked past but never turned down. "I don't know that name. Not like you do."

He says it plainly. No deflection, no retreat. He's looking at the tag now — two fingers held between you, the word facing him, the weight of twelve others still in your pocket. You can see him choosing not to look away.

"These are transport tags," he says. Not a question. He reads it in the shape of them, the way you read a thing you've carried before it was yours. "Twelve units moved and redirected. Not them." He pauses. "Someone wrote that who knew where those units were supposed to go, and decided the destination was wrong."

He looks up at you. The stone in his joints catches the light differently from the way the metal in the fountain does — different source, same material. He is still sitting on the rim. You are still standing. The gap between you is the gap between not-yet-knowing and already-having-known.

"I came out of the desert four days ago," he says. "Maybe five. I don't know H. Voss. But I know what it looks like when someone arrives carrying something they found in another life."

He doesn't ask if you're a returned soul. He doesn't ask what you died as. He just says it, the way he says everything — plainly, like a fact that happens to be true.

"What's written on the other eleven?"

It's not small talk. It's not courtesy. The question is the handle: a stranger on a fountain rim, asking you what you know, because he can see you know something, and the knowing is heavy, and it wants putting down somewhere before it puts you down.

What are the other eleven tags?

## Turn 3

**Player:** I lay the eleven out on the fountain rim between us — not all at once, but one at a time, reading each aloud as I go. Blank. Blank. Blank. A weight in three figures on one, a date that hasn't happened yet on another, a harbor name on a fourth, a single glyph that might be a letter or a unit number on a fifth. The rest are blank. Whoever redirected these knew where they were supposed to go and what was wrong with the destination — but the tags themselves don't say what the cargo was. Only who wasn't supposed to receive it.

I close my hand around the one with *Not them.* "Someone carved that after the cut," I say. "H. Voss. Before they hid the shipment. The rest of the writing — the dates, the weight, the glyphs — that's the empire's hand. Official. But that word is personal."

I look at the figure on the rim with me. "The harbor coordinator. That's who H. Voss was. You said you don't know the name. Do you know the harbor?"

"Blank. Blank. Blank." Your voice is steady on the rim. You read them the way you read a map — not for what it says but for the shape of what it doesn't.

The weight. The date that hasn't happened yet. The harbor name. The glyph that might be a letter or might be a unit number. You lay each one down and the figure on the rim watches each one land.

"Not them."

He says it quietly. He's looking at your hand now, the one closed around the last tag. His eyes went to it the way eyes go to the only lit window in a dark building.

"H. Voss carved that," you say. "After the cut. Before they hid the shipment."

He nods. Slowly. The stone in his joints catches the light differently from the way it does in you — you're still learning to read each other by how the light falls.

"Official hand on the rest," you say. "Empire. But that word is personal."

"The harbor coordinator," you say. "That's who H. Voss was. Do you know the harbor?"

He is quiet for a moment. The water behind you fills the silence.

"I came from the desert four days ago," he says. "Maybe five. I don't know a harbor coordinator. I don't know Lagota's harbor — not by name, not by face. I know the road to Lagota. I know the gate. I know the way the city sits behind its walls like it's holding something in." He pauses. "I know they move things through there. I know the blue stones come from somewhere and go to somewhere and the city is in the middle of it. But the harbor coordinator?" He shakes his head. "That's not my road."

He looks at the eleven tags on the rim.

"But I know what a redirected shipment looks like," he says. "Twelve units. Someone broke the chain and kept the cargo instead of forwarding it. That's not small. That's not casual. Whoever H. Voss was, they didn't just misfile a paperwork error. They stopped a shipment. And they wanted someone to know it was stopped."

He looks at you.

"The inscription says I AM KNOWN," he says. "That's what the wheel turns on. Someone reads it when someone new arrives. That's the system." He taps the tag in your hand — not touching it, but close. "This is someone who stopped the system. Or tried to."

The harbor name sits on the rim between you. The weight, the date, the glyph. Eleven pieces of a picture you don't have the rest of yet.

"The harbor is in Lagota," he says. "If that's where the shipment was supposed to go, that's where the trail is. The question is whether you want to follow it."
