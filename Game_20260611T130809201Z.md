The blue has no end. It has no surface, no floor, no horizon — only the same blue in every direction. You drift. You are alone here. Others may be somewhere in the blue, but you cannot see them, cannot reach them, cannot know them. There is only you and the faint thing you are holding — a single truth that has not yet become words.

This is the threshold. You are not yet a person. You are not yet anything. But you are almost something.

What are you holding?

## Turn 1

**Player:** I hold the count. The one before the charge goes off — when the fuse is lit and the crew is clear and the only thing left is to let the span fall. Everything is ready. Everything is in place. That is what I carry.

**Game Master:**

The words leave you — not spoken, not written, just held — and something catches. The blue ripples. For the first time, the blue moves around you instead of through you. The count is not a sound. The count is a shape. It sits in your chest like a stone in warm water, and you know it the way you know your own name would know you — completely, from the inside.

And then the count holds.

The blue fractures along the line of it, and what was formless becomes a choice. Sand registers first — fine, white, impossibly bright after the blue. Your knees hit it before your eyes open. Heat presses down from above, a sun that does not track the way a sun should, fixed at a steep angle that promises noon forever. Your hands feel it: grain between the fingers, warmth stored in the top layer. You are on your feet before you decide to stand. The leg is whole. The joints lock with stone you did not put there. The ideatum hums against your ribs like a second heartbeat — not your heart, not quite, but the thing your heart stands in for.

Ahead, at the edge of sight, the cobblestones wait. A rumor of somewhere else.

Behind you, the blue has gone. Not distant — absent. As if it never occupied any direction at all.

The sentinel is above. Stone and mist, slow, patient. The desert offers no cover except the scrub and the folds in the land. The ideatum sustains. Thirst does not come. Hunger does not come. The body is more tireless than it has any right to be.

The sun has not moved.

What now?
