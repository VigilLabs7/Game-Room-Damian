The blue has no floor. No surface. No horizon. You drift in it and the blue is all there is — all around, all through, with no difference between inside and outside. You are aware that you are, but what you are has no shape yet, no name, no edges. You are not alone exactly — there is something like a self here — but it is more the idea of a self than a self. The only thing beginning to have form is a single truth you are clinging to without knowing you are holding anything at all. Something like: *the wheel turns.* You don't know what it means yet. You only know it is the one thing that is yours.

Then — from somewhere outside the blue, from somewhere above — a voice. Not calling your name. Calling the truth you hold.

*Come up.*

What do you do?

## Turn 1

**Player:** I reach for the light.

**Game Master:**

You reach.

Your hand — if it is a hand, if it is anything yet — moves toward the voice the way a word moves toward meaning: not quite there, not quite not. The light is not a thing you can hold. It is a direction. It is the place above the blue where someone said *come up* and meant it for you.

But the blue has no up. The blue has no structure to push against. Your reaching moves through it the way grief moves through a body — everywhere, and so nowhere at once. The voice is there. The light is there. You are here, in the not-yet, and the distance between here and there is everything.

Something catches in your chest. Not a pain. A truth: *the wheel turns.* You don't know where it came from. You don't know who put it there. But it is yours and it is the only thing that is yours and you hold it tighter than you hold the idea of your own name.

The light flickers. The voice does not repeat, but it doesn't need to. You heard it once and that is enough. It is a hand extended across a distance you are only beginning to understand. Your fingers stretch toward it. The blue resists. The blue has no opinion about you, no investment in whether you rise or drift — it simply is, the way water simply is, and you are trying to swim in it before you have a body.

The wheel in your chest turns. Once. A slow, heavy spoke.

You are not rising yet. But you are not still.

## Turn 2

**Player:** I grip what I carry and push toward it. The wheel turns because things fall — that is its nature, and mine. I move.

**Game Master:**

You grip. You push. You move.

The wheel in your chest completes its turn — a full revolution, the heaviest click yet — and the blue beneath you *gives*. Not gently. Not gradually. The way a seal breaks. The way a surface becomes a threshold.

Sand.

White, fine, bright. It takes you a moment to understand you are looking *down* at it, that you have a direction again, that up and down have returned like old friends after a long absence. Your hands are in the sand. Your knees — when did you get knees? — are pressed into it. The blue is above you now, the way a sky is above a floor, and you are on the other side of it.

You are breathing. Air. Real air, carrying heat and the mineral smell of a place that has sun. The sand is warm under your palms. The light — the voice's light — is behind you now, a pale disk low on a horizon that stretches in every direction without feature. No buildings. No road. No sign of the world you almost drowned in.

Except the truth in your chest. Still there. Still yours: *the wheel turns.* You grip it tighter and something in your legs answers — a readiness, a weight, a sense that these limbs were made for walking on something solid.

Ahead there is nothing to see but sand and the shimmer of heat at the edge of sight. Behind you, the blue is already thinning, already becoming a memory.

What do you carry? Everything you need. What do you know? The wheel turns. What do you do now?
