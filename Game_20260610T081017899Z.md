The blue has no bottom. You drift in it, weightless, formless — awareness without a body to hold it. Somewhere ahead, or behind, or to the side that doesn't exist yet, a truth sits lodged in the nothing: a single concept you are clinging to without knowing you are clinging to anything at all. The blue presses in from every direction, and you are alone in it, and you have always been alone in it, and the silence here is so complete that even the thought of sound has no meaning.

What do you reach for?

*— What do you reach for?*

## Turn 1

**Player:** I reach for the nine. Not the coins — the weight of them, the count, the shape of what I owe and what I carry. The debt is not a hole; it is the material. I close my fist around it and the blue learns an edge. Every debt is honored. Show me where the first course goes.

**Game Master:**

The blue answers. Not with sound — there is no sound here — but with *resistance*, the way a current meets a hand dipped into it. What you reach for is not empty. The nine sit in the nothing like a counted thing, like a sum already paid or already owed, and when your awareness closes around them they become *more real* than the blue that holds them. The weight of it floods back: not the coins themselves but the *shape* of what you carry, the count of what you owe, the particular gravity of a debt that has not yet been paid or forgiven. You feel it in the space where a chest would be.

The blue learns an edge. Where before it pressed in all directions without texture, without preference, now it has a *shape to avoid* — the shape of what you reached for. You are not formless anymore. You are a form that does not yet know its own edges, holding a shape the blue cannot swallow. You are almost something.

Almost. The surface has not broken. The blue still presses from every direction and you are still drifting in it, aware, orienting, but the reach has given you a *handle* — a counted thing, a weight, a debt acknowledged. The formless watches you with what might be curiosity or patience. You are holding on. You have not yet let go into the world.

What do you do?

## Turn 2

**Player:** I close my fist and push. The nine are my counterweight — I press down on what I carry and the blue gives way beneath me. I fall toward the world, toward the first course. The debt holds me steady as I drop.

**Game Master:**

The fall is not fast.

That is the first thing you know about it — the descent is controlled, deliberate, the way a stone sinks through water rather than the way a body drops through air. The blue parts beneath the weight of what you carry and you go with it, with the nine pressing down and the blue pressing up and the space between them closing around you like a hand closing around a found thing.

The debt holds you steady. That is not a metaphor. The nine are the weight that guides the fall, and the fall is not a surrender — it is a placement. You are being set down. You are being put somewhere. The blue was the space between the world and the idea of the world, and you have crossed it, and what you brought with you is what you are being placed into the world with.

Sand.

Warm. Fine. Real. It takes the shape of where you land — knees, palms, the weight settling, the nine pressing into the grains beside you. The blue is gone. The silence is gone. What replaces them is the sound of wind over flat ground and the immediate, impossible heat of a sun that has not moved since it arrived.

You are on your knees in the desert. The debt is still in your hand. Your other hand presses flat against the sand and you push yourself upright. Your knees are not hurt — there is no leg injury here, no tumble, no sharp arrival. You came down the way a coin comes down when it is placed rather than dropped. The debt honored you as you fell.

Ahead, the sand shifts in the wind. Something glints at the far edge of what you can see — not a structure yet, not a post. A point of light, catching.

You are not barely holding on. You are here.

**What do you do?**

## Turn 3

**Player:** I stand. The sand shifts underfoot and I read it the way I read any footing — looking for where the empire laid the crossing, looking for what was built before this desert was this desert. The glint ahead is a post; I know the type. I brush the nine off and start walking.

**Game Master:**

The sand tells you things if you listen.

Not words — nothing so generous. But the grain has memory in it, and as your feet find their rhythm you read the way a room reads when you enter it blind: where the weight goes, where it pools, where something underneath held its shape before the desert grew over it. The crossings are here. The empire laid them and the desert buried them, but the crossings remember. Your soles read the count of what passed this way and when.

The nine rest easy against your hip. Brushed clean but not forgotten. The weight of them is not a burden — it never was. It is a compass needle that points toward what is owed, and right now it points forward, toward the post, toward whatever the post was set to mark.

The walk is long and the sun does not move.

It hangs at the same impossible angle, fixed in the sky like something borrowed rather than earned. The sand runs pale toward the east and red toward the west and your shadow falls always the same length, never stretching, never shortening. You keep walking. The post grows.

It is a post. Iron-banded, driven deep, the metal collar at its crown green with age. A wooden sign once hung from the crossbar; it is gone now, weathered to nothing or taken. Only the post remains, and from the hook that held the sign something dangles — not a bell, not a token. A small clay figure, sun-bleached nearly white, turning slowly in the heat shimmer.

The glint was the metal collar. The figure was what the light caught on.

Ahead — past the post, past the low ridge it guards — the sand changes color. Darker. Damp. You are close.

**What do you do?**

## Turn 4

**Player:** I take the clay figure down and read it in my palm — sun-bleached, no markings visible, but the shape is deliberate. Someone made this to be looked at. I pocket it and move toward the darker sand, following the debt's compass needle. The damp ahead is the point.

**Game Master:**

The clay figure sits in your palm like a weight that knows its own purpose.

It is smaller than your closed fist. Sun-bleached nearly white, the surface smooth where fingers have worn it, rougher at the edges where they have not. No markings — no score, no stamp, no name. But the shape is deliberate: a figure with arms raised, not in surrender but in balance, the way someone stands when they are holding something heavy and have not yet decided whether to set it down. You turn it once. The weight is more than the size accounts for. Someone made this to be looked at. You pocket it.

The nine press against your hip. Brushed clean but not quiet. The compass needle in them still points forward, and forward is where you go.

The sand changes underfoot. Pale to darker. Dry to damp. The air thickens — not wet exactly, but *present*, the way air gets when water is near and not yet visible. You walk toward it. The compass needle does not waver. It knows where debt lives.

You see the fountain before you reach it.

A line of stone in the heat-haze, then the basin, then the figure on the rim. The fountain is older than the road that will lead you to it. The water rises in a silver column that catches the sun and holds the light. A figure sits on the stone rim with a book open in their lap, pages still, though the breeze is not strong enough to turn them. They watch you approach — not rising, not greeting, just present. The kind of attention that takes inventory.

You stop at the edge of the damp.

The figure closes the book but keeps one finger in it — a habit of someone who was never truly interrupted.

"Someone who wanted it here," they say. Not unkind. Not welcoming. Factual. The water behind them rises and falls in the same unbroken rhythm.

"You're real," they say. "That's the hard part. The rest is just what happens next."

They gesture with the book toward the town behind you. The buildings not quite finished. The street that leads somewhere. "I can tell you what I know about all of it, or I can let you walk into it blind. Your call."

The sun has not moved.

What do you do?
