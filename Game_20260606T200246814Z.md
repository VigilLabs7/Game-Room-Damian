The blue has no bottom. No surface. No horizon. You hang in it and there is nothing to push against, no direction that is more true than another. You are aware that you exist but the shape of you hasn't decided what it is yet. The only thing that has form is the thing you are holding onto without knowing you are holding onto it — a single concept, a single truth, the thing you will become.

The blue moves around you. Not waves. Not currents. Just the endless same-blue, pressing in from every side, patient and total. You are not alone in this place. Every drifter is alone here — even when two are technically nearby they cannot find each other, cannot hear each other, cannot reach. The Ocean does not connect. It only holds.

There is nothing to do but wait. The question is whether you will wait as nothing, or whether the thing you are holding will take weight and become the thing that lifts you out.

You hear something. A voice, maybe. Or the memory of a voice. It is calling from above and it sounds like your idea sounds, like the thing you are clinging to. It says come up, darling.

The blue thins above you. Something is waiting on the other side.

What do you do?

The debt you named — course by course, material built from obligation — meant to be the thing that caught and lifted you. It did not catch. The blue had no surface. The words had no floor.

There is nothing to do, because there is nothing to do it in.

What do you do?

You reach for it and your fingers close on nothing, but nothing has a shape here, and the shape of nothing is what you are trying to fill.

You begin to lay the first course.

It is meticulous work. Each piece of debt — each obligation, each owed, each shape of what you owe — you set it against the dark the way a mason sets stone against stone: precise, deliberate, finding the weak points where nothing meets nothing and filling them. Course by course. The debt as material. You are building a floor in the black, and the floor is not real yet, but the intention of it is real, and in a place where nothing has weight, the intention of a thing may be all that holds.

The shape builds. It does not hold.

It does not collapse — there is nothing here to collapse — but it does not hold either. Each piece you set finds the dark beneath it and goes no further. The floor you are building is a floor-shaped absence. A negative space with the outline of structure. The debt is real but the debt has no floor to rest on and so the debt hangs in the black, held by nothing, held by the act of holding.

You know what is missing. Not the floor — the floor is what you are trying to make. What is missing is the foundation beneath the floor. The thing that makes the first course possible. The weight that lets the second course settle on the first. In a world where nothing holds, the question is not whether you can build. The question is what you build on.

Your hands still hold the shape of the first course. The dark holds nothing.

What do you do?
