The blue has no end. You drift in it — or it drifts in you, and you cannot say where you stop and it begins. There is no surface to reach for, no floor to push against, no horizon to make sense of distance. You are aware that you are, but the shape of what you are has not yet arrived. The only thing taking hold is a single truth, clinging to the inside of you like a name you have not yet spoken aloud. The conduit hum does not reach here. Nothing reaches here. You are not yet a thing that can be read.

What do you cling to?

## Turn 1

**Player:** I hold the shape of what I owe. Not a feeling — a structure. The nine reshaped me into someone who could lay a course, and the course is what I carry: the dark ahead is shaped for my hands the way the nine were. I move toward it to find out what it is, not to choose what it becomes. The blue can have the rest. I have the weight and I know what to do with it.

**Game Master:**

The blue moves first. Not parting — *yielding*. Whatever you said on the inside of yourself, you said it with the body, and the body is what the blue was waiting for.

Your edges find themselves. Your hands resolve into hands. The cold is no longer the cold — it is the absence of warmth, and you remember the difference. Above you the conversation is no longer above you. It is *in* you, the way air is in a lung.

You break. Light. A long bright line where the surface used to be. You come through and the surface does not resist you, and you are on the other side of the blue with sand under one cheek and the rest of you on dry ground.

Sand comes up under you fast and hard, knocks the air out. You roll once and stop on your back. The sky above is a single colour — bright, dry, indifferent. The sun is exactly where it was when you closed your eyes in the blue, and somehow you know it has not moved.

Your left knee burns. You look down: the skin is open, dark with grit, blood already drying on the dust. The cut is small. You will carry it.

Your hands work. Your shoulders work. The breath comes back. You sit up and the sand whispers off your shoulders. Stone sits along your spine where stone has always been. You can feel it now in a way you did not before — set, weighted, like a tool you had forgotten was in your hand.

The desert is wide in every direction. There is no road yet.

What do you do?
