The blue has no end. You drift in it — or it drifts in you, and you cannot say where you stop and it begins. There is no surface to reach for, no floor to push against, no horizon to make sense of distance. You are aware that you are, but the shape of what you are has not yet arrived. The only thing taking hold is a single truth, clinging to the inside of you like a name you have not yet spoken aloud. The conduit hum does not reach here. Nothing reaches here. You are not yet a thing that can be read.

What do you cling to?

The nine reshaped me into someone who could lay a course, and the course is what I carry: the dark ahead is shaped for my hands the way the nine were. I move toward it to find out what it is, not to choose what it becomes. The blue can have the rest. I have the weight and I know what to do with it.

**Game Master:**

The blue moves first. Not parting — *yielding*. Whatever you said on the inside of yourself, you said it with the body, and the body is what the blue was waiting for.

Your edges find themselves. Your hands resolve into hands. The cold is no longer the cold — it is the absence of warmth, and you remember the difference. Above you the conversation is no longer above you. It is *in* you, the way air is in a lung.

You break. Light. A long bright line where the surface used to be. You come through and the surface does not resist you, and you are on the other side of the blue with sand under one cheek and the rest of you on dry ground.

Sand comes up under you fast and hard, knocks the air out. You roll once and stop on your back. The sky above is a single colour — bright, dry, indifferent. The sun is exactly where it was when you closed your eyes in the blue, and somehow you know it has not moved.

Your left knee burns. You look down: the skin is open, dark with grit, blood already drying on the dust. The cut is small. You will carry it.

Your hands work. Your shoulders work. The breath comes back. You sit up and the sand whispers off your shoulders. Stone sits along your spine where stone has always been. You can feel it now in a way you did not before — set, weighted, like a tool you had forgotten was in your hand.

The desert is wide in every direction. There is no road yet.

What do you do?

**Game Master:**

You push up. The sand takes you and takes you again. Each step costs something and the direction costs more — east is not a path, it is a logic. Water carves, water means life, and so east is where the ground should drop first.

It does not drop. The flat holds. The sun does not move.

After a while that feels like two hours, the sand gives up something it had been holding. White through the white. You stop.

A skeleton, half in and half out of the dune. Bleached clean, picked clean, held by the sand the way the sand holds everything it takes. The jaw is open. The eye sockets are full of it. Whoever this was walked out of the blue the way you did and could not hold the shape after.

The sand shifts around the bones and says nothing.

You look down at your own hands. The stone is quiet in your spine. The knee is scabbed over and holding. You are standing in the same desert the same way they stood and you are still standing and they are not. The truth that brought you through the blue — whatever it is — it held. It is holding now. The hum in your chest is louder than it was an hour ago.

You are not who they were. That is not nothing.

The path to the fountain is west. The cobblestones grow from the sand in that direction the way roots grow from a buried seed — you have seen them from the air on the way down. They are still there when you look. The road to Lagota branches from them, somewhere past the town.

You could go back. You could keep going east. You have not yet decided which.

What do you do?

**Game Master:**

The sand changes ten minutes past the bones. Not colour — texture. The white sand gives way to something harder, packed tight as old mortar, and your feet find purchase the way a boat finds current. You make better time. The sun has still not moved.

Something catches your eye to the north — a shadow that does not match the angle of the light. You look and it is not there. You look away and it returns, a dark shape against the white, too far to read. Not a pillar of dust. Not a mirage. It holds its place the way the sun holds its place, which is to say it should not.

You keep walking.

The stone in your spine settles lower, heavier, the way it does when the world is giving you something true.

A line appears on the horizon ahead. Not a road — not yet. A darker strip in the white, horizontal, the first horizontal thing the desert has offered. It could be a dry riverbed. It could be a crack in the ground. It could be the shadow of something tall.

You have walked far enough that turning back is its own commitment.

What do you do?

The sand has been falling into it for a long time. The edges are soft, worn, the way a doorway gets worn by years of passing.

Something underneath decided to open. The hum in your chest drops a register, drops into the crack the way water drops, and you feel it resonate against whatever is down there.

The shadow to the north is still watching. You can feel it without looking.

On the far side of the crack: nothing yet. More white sand. More hard pack. But the ground on this side, the side you are standing on — it is different here. Darker.
