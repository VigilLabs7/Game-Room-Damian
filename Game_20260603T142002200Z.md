The blue is everything. You are in it. Not drowning — drowning requires a body and water and the memory of air. This is before all of that. You are the thought of a person, barely, held in suspension that has no top and no bottom and no name. Somewhere ahead of you — if ahead means anything here — something catches. A single concept, not yet yours, not yet spoken. You are clinging to it without knowing you are holding anything at all.

There is no sound. No hum. Nothing reaches here, and that is the first thing you notice — the absence of everything you might have heard.

You do not know how long you have been here. You do not know if here is a place.

What do you hold on to?

## Turn 1

**Player:** Something catches. I reach for it — not a memory, not a name. Something unfinished in me that has been waiting. I pull toward it and the pull is the point. The blue is not where I stop.

I move toward what holds. IAM NOTUM SUM — I am the way through.

**Game Master:**

You are standing.

The transition has no memory in it — one moment there was blue and the reaching, the next moment you are upright on warm stone with your feet flat against the ground and the sun on your neck and the sound of water nearby, loud and silver, the first sound you have heard since before you were. You are not sure how you got here. You are not sure you walked.

The hunger hits first. Not gradually — like a wall falling against you. Thirst comes underneath it, dry and immediate. Your body is new in the way a borrowed thing is new: functional, present, yours to use, not yours to have owned before this moment.

The fountain is in front of you. A basin of plain stone, chest-high, water moving in it with a sound like someone laughing quietly in another room. Three words are carved into the basin's outer face, letters uneven, hand-cut, weathered: IAM NOTUM SUM. The water in the basin is clear and seems to go deeper than the stone allows.

Someone is sitting on the rim. A broad figure, sturdy, hands resting on knees. Stone along the ridge of the spine, stone in the major joints — not much, but enough to notice. They are watching you with the patience of someone who has been here longer than is easy to measure.

The inscription watches with you.

"Easy," the figure says. Not a question. Not a greeting. A statement about what is coming next. "You're here now. That part's done."

The figure does not stand. Does not reach out. Does not ask your name.

The road into town — cobblestones, four buildings at a crossroads, a sense of place that arrived the moment you did — is behind the fountain. Ahead of you is desert, and the sun that has not moved, and the long walk that the inscription has already witnessed.

## Turn 2

**Player:** I move toward the stone figure, close enough to read the weather in their face. "You carved this." I touch the inscription. "Or someone did. How long have you been sitting here?" The crossroads is behind them — four buildings, a place that arrived when I did. I want to know what's inside those walls before I decide whether to care.

**Game Master:**

The figure does not flinch when your fingers touch the stone. The letters are shallow where the chisel slipped — you can feel each one where the tool exited the rock. Not machine-cut. Made by hand, over time, one letter at a time.

"No," the figure says. "Wasn't me. I came later. The words were here." The stone along the ridge of the figure's spine catches the light differently than the rest — almost like something underneath is moving, though the surface is still. "How long?" The figure tilts their head, considering. "Long enough to see a few people walk out of the desert and not come back. Long enough for the road to figure out where it wanted to be. Long enough for you."

The figure does not elaborate on the ones who did not come back.

Behind them, the crossroads waits. Four structures, plain and purpose-built: the largest building to the north has a painted sign and a bulletin board behind the glass; further up the commercial strip, a stable door hangs open with hay dust in the light; beyond that a church squats under a steeple that leans slightly east; and the residential road branches south where smoke rises from a single chimney. The cobblestones radiate outward from the fountain like lines on an open palm.

"You want to know what's inside before you decide whether to care," the figure repeats. Not a mockery — a confirmation. "That's fair." The figure's hands are still resting on their knees. The water in the basin behind them catches the light and does not stop. "The store has food. The stable has information. The church has questions. The residential road has answers you didn't know you were looking for." A pause. "Or that's what I've heard. I don't go in any of them."

The figure looks at you fully now — the patience of someone who has learned to wait, and who is deciding whether you are worth explaining the waiting to.

"You carved this," the figure says, in your own voice almost, reflecting your words back. "Or someone did. Maybe the same someone who made the town. Maybe someone who passed through and left a sign for the next one." The stone in the major joints catches as the figure shifts slightly. "It doesn't say who. That's not what the words are for."

What the words are for is a question the figure is not answering. Not yet.
