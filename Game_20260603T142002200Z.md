The blue is everything. You are in it. Not drowning — drowning requires a body and water and the memory of air. This is before all of that. You are the thought of a person, barely, held in suspension that has no top and no bottom and no name. Somewhere ahead of you — if ahead means anything here — something catches. A single concept, not yet yours, not yet spoken. You are clinging to it without knowing you are holding anything at all.

There is no sound. No hum. Nothing reaches here, and that is the first thing you notice — the absence of everything you might have heard.

You do not know how long you have been here. You do not know if here is a place.

What do you hold on to?

**Game Master:**

You are standing.

The transition has no memory in it — one moment there was blue and the reaching, the next moment you are upright on warm stone with your feet flat against the ground and the sun on your neck and the sound of water nearby, loud and silver, the first sound you have heard since before you were. You are not sure how you got here. You are not sure you walked.

The hunger hits first. Not gradually — like a wall falling against you. Thirst comes underneath it, dry and immediate. Your body is new in the way a borrowed thing is new: functional, present, yours to use, not yours to have owned before this moment.

The fountain is in front of you. A basin of plain stone, chest-high, water moving in it with a sound like someone laughing quietly in another room. Three words are carved into the basin's outer face, letters uneven, hand-cut, weathered: IAM NOTUM SUM. The water in the basin is clear and seems to go deeper than the stone allows.

Someone is sitting on the rim. A broad figure, sturdy, hands resting on knees. Stone along the ridge of the spine, stone in the major joints — not much, but enough to notice. They are watching you with the patience of someone who has been here longer than is easy to measure.

The inscription watches with you.

"Easy," the figure says. Not a question. Not a greeting. A statement about what is coming next. "You're here now. That part's done."

The figure does not stand. Does not reach out. Does not ask your name.

The road into town — cobblestones, four buildings at a crossroads, a sense of place that arrived the moment you did — is behind the fountain. Ahead of you is desert, and the sun that has not moved, and the long walk that the inscription has already witnessed.
