The blue has no end. No floor, no surface, no horizon — only the same in every direction, and you in it, or the idea of you, drifting. You are not cold. You are not warm. You are not anything yet, except the thing you are holding: a single truth you cannot name because naming would mean knowing what it is, and you do not yet. You only know it is yours.

The blue thins somewhere above you. Not a direction — the blue itself becomes less itself, and through the thinning comes something that might be a voice, might be a silence shaped like a voice, calling upward. Your truth catches on it. A log in water. Something growing branches.

What do you reach for?

Not for the voice. For what the voice is moving through.

The blue gives. Not all at once — it bends, thins, pulls apart like something being breathed. Through the gap you feel it: the shape of what called you, reaching back the way a hand reaches through water toward something it cannot quite close around. Your truth catches on it. Branches where there was only blue. Growth where there was only formless.

You hold. The voice presses through you — not into you, through you, the way a river presses through a channel cut in stone. You feel what it carries: the shape of a world being made, a name that is yours and not yet spoken, a truth you cannot read because you have not yet become the one who holds it. It is yours. It is almost yours. It is not quite in your hands yet.

The blue steadies around you. Not gone — the same blue in every direction, floorless, endless — but you are no longer drifting in it. You are holding. The voice is still calling, still thin, still almost-light above you. The branches are still growing.

The blue releases you. Not a shattering — a giving way, the way a held breath finally lets go, and you are through it.

Sand takes you. It pulls at your ankles, your calves, warm against skin that has forgotten what temperature means. The sun is directly overhead. It has not moved. You notice this. You file it under impossible things.

Your left leg folds under you before you can account for it. A wet red thread running from knee to ankle — the kind that means you tumbled out of somewhere with force you did not slow. The flesh remembers the Ocean even if the mind has already left. You are bleeding. You are in a body. You are in the world.

The sand extends in every direction without feature, without landmark, the same in all directions until something in the distance — not close, not far, just *present* in the way the desert is always present — catches the light. A structure. A fountain. A town.

You need to walk toward it. But right now you need to learn what this body can do first — what the heat demands, what the wound wants.

You pull yourself upright. The thing you are carrying settles into your chest like it was always meant to fit there. Its truth surfaces: *commerce is the truce*. Not yet a complete thought — more like a shape you are still learning to hold. You have it. You took it. It is yours.

The sand shifts. The sun has not moved. The structure waits.
