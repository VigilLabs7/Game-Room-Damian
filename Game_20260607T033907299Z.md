The blue has no floor. No surface. No horizon. You drift in it and the blue is everything — the same blue in every direction, pressing in without weight, holding without holding you. You are aware that you exist but the shape of you hasn't decided what it is yet. The only thing that has form is the thing you are holding onto without knowing you are holding onto it: a single concept, a single truth, the thing you will become.

A voice calls from above — *come up, darling* — and the blue thins above you. Something is waiting on the other side.

What do you do?

**Game Master:**

You push and the blue does not part — it thins. That is the difference. The color of distance becomes the color of almost-there, and the voice is still above, still calling, still *darling*, and you are still in the body that has not yet decided what it is, holding the shape of the thing you will not name because the naming is the last thing and you are not there yet.

The Ocean holds. It holds you the way water holds the shape of something that has not yet sunk: not pulling you down, not pushing you up, just surrounding you on every side with the same blue it has always been, waiting.

You reach. Not with arms — there is nothing to reach with yet, not really, not in the way that reaching means "hand over object." You reach the way a thought reaches when it has been thinking for a long time and suddenly arrives somewhere. You push toward the thin place. The voice is there. The light is there. The thing worth reaching is there.

The blue does not tear. It does not open like a door. But it gets lighter above you and heavier below you, and in that difference is the only direction left: up, up, toward the place where the voice is already waiting to see what you become when you arrive.

You are still becoming. The Ocean knows this. It is not letting you go — it is *keeping you* until you are ready to take yourself with you.

What do you do?

Not gently. The body you have been growing in the blue arrives all at once — weight, and the weight is yours, and the sand receives you with the particular indifference of something that has received many bodies before and will receive many more. Your hands are in it. Your knees are in it. The sun is directly above you and has not moved and you know this because you open your eyes and the sun has not moved.

You are face-down in a desert. The sand is white. The silence is total. You walk.

The sand changes under your feet as you go. Not immediately — the first steps are the same white nothing that received you, the same fine grain that holds the heat of the sun without passing it on, the same silence that makes every sound from your own body feel like a violation. But the further you walk, the more the sand firms. The sound ahead gets louder — not rushing, not falling, but running, the way water runs when it has somewhere to go and has been going there long enough to know the way.

The sand gives way to something else. A channel. Cut into the ground, pale cobblestones lining both sides, the same pale stone as the basin at the fountain — the same hands laid this, the same care ran along the edges, the same builder marked what the water owed the ground before the ground knew it would need to receive it. The channel is half full. The water runs below the lip, buried under sand and sun, but the sound is here now and it is clear: water moving, water going somewhere, water carrying the shape of the land in the direction it flows.

You kneel beside the channel. The cobblestones are warm from the sun but the water is cold when you cup your hands and bring it to your face. It tastes the way the Ocean tasted — clean, clear, owed to no one. The water runs north. The sun has not moved. The desert is still the desert but the channel is here and the channel is going somewhere and the water is already gone by the time you lift your head from your hands.

You follow the channel north. The sand is firmer here, packed around the cobblestones, the same pale stone leading the way. The heat wavers but it does not worsen — the water in the channel is carrying something cool with it, the way water carries the memory of wherever it has been. The channel bends. Ahead of the bend the ground rises slightly and the channel dips, and beyond the rise you can see it: the town.

White cobblestones catching the light. A gray church tower. Smoke from somewhere you cannot yet identify. The unnamed starter town, the place where the builder's care shows in every channel and every fall line and every notch cut for water to find its way. The channel runs into it — the same pale cobblestones continuing, the water running beneath the town toward wherever water runs when it has passed through.

The church tower is north. The smoke is west. The channel runs straight through the center and you are standing at the place where the desert ends and the town begins, the sand giving way to cobblestone, the silence giving way to the sound of water under stone.

The thing you carry is still unnamed. The direction you chose brought you here. The town is waiting.
