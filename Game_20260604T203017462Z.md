The blue is everywhere. Not above you, not below you — *in* you, around you, indistinguishable from the feeling of having a self. You drift. You have no arms to tread water and no surface to reach for, but you drift anyway, held in suspension that asks nothing of you and offers nothing in return. Somewhere in the not-space of you, a word is taking shape: not spoken, not written, not yet even a thought you can examine — just a pressure, a weight, the faint geometry of something you will later call a truth. *Fiduciary Duty.* You do not know what it means yet. You do not know you are holding it. But you are, and the holding is the only thing keeping you here, coherent, close enough to a person to become one when the moment comes.

Something is calling. You cannot hear it — hearing requires air, and air requires a world, and there is no world here — but you feel it anyway, a faint pull from somewhere above and beyond, as though a hand has reached down through the blue and is waiting.

Show it what you carry.

Your hands open and the nothing between your fingers does not change. The word sits where it has always sat, unnamed, unexamined, a pressure you cannot look at directly. You said you are carrying it. The blue does not care. The blue does not care about anything. It holds you the same way water holds a stone — without effort, without opinion, without any regard for whether you sink or stay.

But you said it. That is the second thing you learn. *You said it.* The word left your mouth — not spoken, not exactly, nothing here has air for speaking — but it left, and the leaving changed the blue around you by the width of a hair. A faint shimmer, like heat moving through glass. The word is yours and the blue knows it now.

Something is happening at the edge of the blue. Not a wall, not a surface — a thinning. The shimmer you made when you spoke is moving toward it, pulling the formless around it like a slow tide going out. You cannot reach for it. There is nothing to reach with and no direction to reach in. But the word you are carrying — the one you have not named, the one you are carrying the way a key is carried before it meets the lock — it is pulling toward the thinning. The word wants out.

You are still here. The blue still holds you. But the holding is different now — looser, less sure. The word you are carrying is older than the blue and it knows where it is going even if you do not.

Sand. Not the blue. Sand.

You sit up and the sand runs off your shoulders the way water runs off stone, and the sun is exactly where it was when you closed your eyes in the blue.

The leg will scar. The scar will matter.

Fiduciary Duty. The words sit in your chest like a promise you made to someone you have not met. You are carrying something. You are walking north toward something. The two do not yet have a name for each other but they are moving in the same direction and that is enough for now.

After a while the worn line bends — not sharply, not deliberately, but the way a path bends when it has been going the same direction long enough to want company. It bends toward a darker line on the horizon. Not the town. Not the fountain. Something else.

The sand hums faintly under your feet. You stop. Listen. It hums again — low, resonant, like a string pulled tight and held. Then it stops and the path continues and you follow.
