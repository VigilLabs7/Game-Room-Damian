The blue is everywhere. Not above you, not below you — *in* you, around you, indistinguishable from the feeling of having a self. You drift. You have no arms to tread water and no surface to reach for, but you drift anyway, held in suspension that asks nothing of you and offers nothing in return. Somewhere in the not-space of you, a word is taking shape: not spoken, not written, not yet even a thought you can examine — just a pressure, a weight, the faint geometry of something you will later call a truth. *Fiduciary Duty.* You do not know what it means yet. You do not know you are holding it. But you are, and the holding is the only thing keeping you here, coherent, close enough to a person to become one when the moment comes.

Something is calling. You cannot hear it — hearing requires air, and air requires a world, and there is no world here — but you feel it anyway, a faint pull from somewhere above and beyond, as though a hand has reached down through the blue and is waiting.

Show it what you carry.

## Turn 1

**Player:** I open my hands. Whatever the word is, I am carrying it across.

**Game Master:**

The blue does not answer. That is the first thing you learn here — the blue does not answer, and waiting for it to is a kind of drowning dressed in stillness. Your hands open and the nothing between your fingers does not change. The word sits where it has always sat, unnamed, unexamined, a pressure you cannot look at directly. You said you are carrying it. The blue does not care. The blue does not care about anything. It holds you the same way water holds a stone — without effort, without opinion, without any regard for whether you sink or stay.

But you said it. That is the second thing you learn. *You said it.* The word left your mouth — not spoken, not exactly, nothing here has air for speaking — but it left, and the leaving changed the blue around you by the width of a hair. A faint shimmer, like heat moving through glass. The word is yours and the blue knows it now.

Something is happening at the edge of the blue. Not a wall, not a surface — a thinning. The shimmer you made when you spoke is moving toward it, pulling the formless around it like a slow tide going out. You cannot reach for it. There is nothing to reach with and no direction to reach in. But the word you are carrying — the one you have not named, the one you are carrying the way a key is carried before it meets the lock — it is pulling toward the thinning. The word wants out.

You are still here. The blue still holds you. But the holding is different now — looser, less sure. The word you are carrying is older than the blue and it knows where it is going even if you do not.

What do you reach for?

## Turn 2

**Player:** I do not reach. Reaching is something you do with arms and I have none here. I let the word carry me — fiduciary duty, the weight I did not choose and cannot put down. I stop fighting the holding. I let the thinning take what it wants.

**Game Master:**

The word moves first.

That is the first thing you know that you did not know before — the word is not inside you, you are inside the word, and the word has decided to go. Fiduciary Duty. The weight you did not choose and cannot put down. It rises through you the way a key rises through water, not because it is light but because it has somewhere to be, and you go with it.

The blue does not part. The blue *agrees.* There is a difference. Parting is something that happens to boundaries; agreeing is what the blue does when it recognizes something it has been holding for someone else. The thinning opens and the opening is not empty — it is full of the thing you are carrying, and the thing you are carrying is where you need to go.

Sand. Not the blue. Sand.

You fall once and hard. The impact is brief and total — your left knee takes the weight of you and the skin opens where the grit gets in. You do not feel it as pain yet. You will. You sit up and the sand runs off your shoulders the way water runs off stone, and the sun is exactly where it was when you closed your eyes in the blue.

The leg will scar. The scar will matter.

You are on the other side.

Sand holds. That is the first true thing in this place. Your knee takes the weight and the grit holds and the thing you are carrying stays where it is — in your chest, behind your ribs, the way a coin sits in a pocket before someone spends it.

You stand and the world arranges itself around you. The sun is low on one side, which means west is that direction and east is the other. In front of you the sand flattens into something that might be a path — not paved, not made, but *worn*, the way ground becomes road when enough feet have crossed it going the same way. South, toward where the sun is going, the desert opens wide. The light there is full and even. It looks like the kind of place where decisions are simple.

North, the dunes rise. Darker sand, tighter grain, the kind of ground that makes you work for every step. The path there is less clear. The path there is for someone who already knows what they are doing.

You do not know what you are doing yet. You are standing in the space between the choice and the move, holding the weight of a word you did not choose and cannot put down. *Fiduciary Duty.* It sits in you like a duty — like something owed, something held in trust for someone else. You do not know who you are holding it for. Not yet.

The sand around you has no opinion. The sun has not moved. You are the only thing here with a will, and the will has not decided.

Which way?
