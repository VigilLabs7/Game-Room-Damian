The blue has no bottom. No surface. No current. You drift in it and the blue is you and you are the blue and there is nothing else — not darkness, not light, just the sense of being suspended in something without edges. You are aware that you are. You are not yet sure what that means.

Something is forming. Not a shape — a weight. A single concept pressing against the inside of what you are, like a word on the tip of a tongue you don't yet have. It is the only thing in the blue that is yours. You hold it without knowing you hold it.

There is no sound. No voice yet. No one else. The blue is absolute and you are alone in it in a way that has no remedy, because there is nothing here but you and the thing you're gripping without knowing you're gripping it.

What do you hold? What is the one truth you would take with you if you had to choose?

**Game Master:**

You plant your feet and the world plants you back.

The blue does not release you gently. It tears — the way plaster cracks when the wall beneath it shifts. Then air — hot, dry, nothing like the blue. Then weight: the full gravity of a body you did not have a moment ago, now dragging at you from the inside, your own mass returned to you like a debt you forgot you owed.

You hit the ground and the ground is real.

The leg screams. You look down and the left one is wrong — the angle of it, the heat spreading from the ankle where the bone did not quite set right when the formless spat you out. The blue gave you a body and the body came with damage. This is the price of arrival. You have seen others make this passage. The nine taught you that the leg heals crooked if you do not set it clean, and the empire's healers charge more than a bridge is worth.

You drag yourself upright. The sand is white and fine and goes nowhere — no horizon, no landmark, just the light coming down even and merciless from a sun that has not moved since anyone started watching it. Somewhere ahead — the only direction that matters — you can hear water. Not rushing. Not falling. Just the sound of something still, held in one place, waiting.

You walk toward it. The leg holds. Barely. Each step a negotiation between what you know the body can do and what the body is willing to give. You are a builder. You know load-bearing structures. This ankle is not load-bearing anymore but you are asking it to bear anyway, and it is answering — unwilling, grudging, but answering.

The sound of water sharpens. Then the fountain resolves through the heat-haze: stone basin, clear water, and a figure sitting on its edge with their back to you, watching the road that leads into town.

The road has four landmarks. You can see two from here — a general store with a faded awning, and something taller behind it, the church's spire catching the light.

The figure has not turned. They are waiting for you to close the distance.
