The blue is everything. Before you, nothing. Behind you, nothing. There is no surface to reach for, no floor to push against — only the same blue in every direction, and the slow awareness that you are here, that you exist, that something inside you is holding on even though you cannot yet name what it is.

You drift. You do not know for how long. There is no time here that works the way time works in the world above — only the sense of being, of persisting, of not yet being gone. And then, faintly, something begins to take shape. A concept. A principle. A single truth that is becoming more real the longer you hold it — not yet a word, not yet a name, but the shape of what you will carry when the blue lets go.

A voice finds you. Not from below. From somewhere above the blue entirely — warm, deliberate, intimate. *Come up, darling.* Your ideatum catches the call like a hand catching a rope. You feel yourself beginning to rise. The blue thins. The color bleeds toward white, toward gold, toward heat.

You break the surface into something new. Sand presses against skin you did not know you had. A sun the size of a judgment burns straight down from a sky with no memory of clouds. The world is here. It has been waiting.

You are what you carried up with you. What is that?

**What do you do?**

## Turn 1

**Game Master:**

The sand holds. You expected it might not — expected the blue to swallow you the moment you reached for something solid — but the grains press back against your palm with the patience of something that has been here forever. Your knees sink slightly. Your weight is real. The horizon is a line drawn sharp between sand and sky, and nothing moves along it.

The sun is overhead and enormous. It does not track across the dome of the sky the way suns do — it sits at the apex like a single burning eye, white-gold and merciless. The sand around your knees is the color of old bone. There is no wind. There is no sound except the faint grain-shift when you shift your weight, and the harder fact of your own breathing, which you can now hear because you have ears, because you have a body, because the Ocean let go.

You scan the horizon. Heat mirages warp the distant line where earth meets sky — nothing structural, nothing living, just the shimmer of a world that has not yet decided what it is. Your hands are new. The lines in your palms are unread. You have fingers and they work and that is, for now, enough.

A glint. Faint, low on the southern horizon — something catching the sun's blaze at an angle. Not a mirage. Something solid, left in the sand. Broken sign, maybe. Wreckage of a prior arrival. Or something else entirely.

The sun has not moved. It will not move. But your throat is already dry and the heat is already pulling at the edges of you.

**What do you do?**

## Turn 2

**Game Master:**

You walk. The sand holds its breath beneath you — firm, packed, as if the desert itself chose to be solid for you this once. Each step drives your heel into a surface that does not give, and the effort of it registers in your thighs, your calves, the new architecture of a body you are still learning to trust. The sun does not move. The light it casts comes straight down, flattening every shadow until there is nothing to hide in, nothing between you and the full weight of the sky.

The glint sharpens as you close the distance. Not a reflection — a edge. Something standing upright in the sand at an angle that catches the sun's blaze and throws it back in a single bright pulse, regular, rhythmic. Deliberate. You read the geometry as you walk: two legs, maybe, and a body between them. A figure. Small at this distance, but not a sign. Not wreckage. Something shaped.

The sand begins to change color a few dozen steps before you reach it — the pale bone tone darkening to something greyer, damper, as if the ground itself wept here once and the stain never fully dried. You feel the temperature drop before you see why. The heat pulls back like a curtain drawn aside. The air here is not cool but it is not burning. It is the difference between an oven and a room.

The glint resolves into a person.

They are sitting in the sand with their back to you, facing south, utterly still. Their clothes are dark — darker than the wet ground beneath them — and their head is bowed as if in thought or prayer or exhaustion. They do not move when you approach. The silence here is different from the silence of the open desert. It has texture. Weight.

Your shadow falls across them. They still do not turn.

Something in you recognizes the shape of their stillness. The angle of their shoulders. The particular quality of a person who is waiting for something they have been waiting for a very long time.

You are ten paces away. The heat presses at your back. The figure waits, face toward the southern horizon, unaware — or pretending to be unaware — that you are here.
