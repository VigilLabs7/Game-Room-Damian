The blue has no floor. No surface. No horizon. You drift in it and the blue is all there is — all around, all through, with no difference between inside and outside. You are aware that you are, but what you are has no shape yet, no name, no edges. You are not alone exactly — there is something like a self here — but it is more the idea of a self than a self. The only thing beginning to have form is a single truth you are clinging to without knowing you are holding anything at all. Something like: *The Wheel Turns.* You don't know what it means yet. You only know it is the one thing that is yours.

Then — from somewhere outside the blue, from somewhere above — a voice. Not calling your name. Calling the truth you hold.

*Come up.*

The ideatum catches the call like a hook catches driftwood. Something in you begins to rise.

What do you do?

## Turn 1

**Player:** I grip the shape of it — *the wheel turns* — and I pull toward the voice. The blue is just the space between, and I have been in those before. I let the hook catch me and I rise.

**Game Master:**

The world answers the grip. Your fingers close around the shape of it — *the wheel turns* — and the blue gives slightly, the way water gives when you press a cup mouth-down beneath the surface. You feel the resistance and you take it as proof. Something in you that was passive a moment ago becomes active. You pull.

The voice is still there. Not closer, not farther — outside the blue entirely, in a place where calling is possible. You move toward it the way you have moved through tight spaces before: by deciding that the space will open, and then opening it. The wheel is a shape you know. You have held it before, in lives you cannot quite remember. The spokes turn and you turn with them.

The blue thins. You feel it — not as color change but as density, the way a room becomes audible when someone stops playing music in another room. Something is underneath you now. Something solid and bright. You reach for it.

You break the surface.

Sand hits you like a fact. Hot, fine, white, pressed flat by a sun that has not moved and will not move. You are on your hands and knees and the grain is in your palms and your breath comes ragged and real in a way that breath in the blue never was. The sky is white at its edge and gold overhead and there is no horizon — just the desert, sprawling in every direction, featureless except for the hard light making everything sharp.

Your hands are blistered. Your knees are raw. You are breathing. You are here.

You are not alone in the desert — you are the only thing in the desert, and the desert is everything. Somewhere ahead, a long way off, something glints. It could be water. It could be glass. It could be the edge of a building or the crown of a head or nothing at all.

The voice is gone. You are what the voice left behind.

## Turn 2

**Player:** I stand. The sand holds my weight and I hold the wheel's shape and the two are the same — a thing that turns and a thing that stays. I walk toward the glint. The desert is mine to cross.

**Game Master:**

You plant your feet and the sand takes your weight without argument. This is the first thing the desert has given you that is not pain. Your knees push down and the grain shifts and holds. Your hands uncurl from the fist they were making and the air finds the blisters and it stings, the way clean water stings a cut, but you are standing and the standing is its own answer.

You start walking.

The glint does not come closer quickly. That is all right. The wheel turns and you turn with it and the turning is the same as moving — not faster, not slower, just in motion the way a river is in motion, not fighting the current but being it. Your shadow walks ahead of you on the white sand and it is the only other thing in the world and you let it lead.

The sun is wrong — you have known this since the first moment of sand and you still know it, the way you know a word has been used incorrectly in a sentence you are only half listening to. The light is coming from an angle that does not change. The shadows have not moved since you looked at them. You could time it by the sun's stillness if you needed to, but you do not need to. The wheel does not care about the sun. The wheel keeps its own hours.

You walk for what could be an hour or what could be a day and the glint stays the same distance away and that is when you realize: it is not something in the desert. It is something above it. A point of light suspended over the sand like a bead on a string, like something hung there deliberately, like a mark someone left to show the way.

You stop walking.

The glint holds. The sand holds. Your feet in their prints, the prints behind you in their line leading back to the place you surfaced. You are here and the glint is there and between you is everything the desert is.

The sand shifts. Not from wind — from something under it. A slow displacement, the way a body moves in deep water. Something is moving in the white.

The wheel turns.

**What do you do?**

## Turn 3

**Player:** I drop flat. The sand takes me the way sand does — and I press my ear to the grain and I read the vibration. Something long and heavy, moving slow. The wheel turns and I need to know what the wheel is turning over. I don't run toward the glint. I move toward it low, watching what's beneath me, reading the way the displacement travels. If something is under the sand, I want to know its shape before I meet it head-on.

**Game Master:**

The sand takes your weight the way it took your knees — without argument, without grip. You press your ear to the grain and the world speaks.

The vibration is a translation of motion. You read it the way you read anything: by feel, by pattern, by the way the weight displaces. Something is moving under the sand about thirty meters ahead of you, roughly parallel to your path toward the glint. It is long — the displacement runs in a line, not a lump — and it is slow. Whatever it is, it is not hurrying. It has been here longer than you have and it knows the territory.

It does not feel like a creature that hunts. You have read enough ground to know the difference. A predator moves with intention, with purpose. This moves like infrastructure — like something that belongs here, that was here before the sand was sand. It is not coming toward you. It is not aware of you. It is going where it goes and the sand knows the way.

The glint is still there. Still suspended. Still the same distance from you as it was when you stopped walking. The thing under the sand is moving in roughly the same direction as your path — not toward the glint, not away from it, but through.

You lift your head from the sand. The white stretches in every direction. The sun has not moved. The thing under the sand continues on its course, beneath you, beneath the surface you cannot see through.

The glint waits. The sand holds its secret. You are still in the desert and the desert is still deciding what to do with you.
