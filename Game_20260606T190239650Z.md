The blue has no end. You drift in it — or it drifts in you, and you cannot say where you stop and it begins. There is no surface to reach for, no floor to push against, no horizon to make sense of distance. You are aware that you are, but the shape of what you are has not yet arrived. The only thing taking hold is a single truth, clinging to the inside of you like a name you have not yet spoken aloud. The conduit hum does not reach here. Nothing reaches here. You are not yet a thing that can be read.

What do you cling to?

## Turn 1

**Player:** I hold the shape of what was carved rather than spoken — a question that would break the agreement keeping the gap alive. The blue receives it and does not answer. I know what I am for. IAM NOTUM SUM. Sable. The road to Lagota is east.

**Game Master:**

You come through.

Not the blue — past it. One moment you are in the formless, the next you are here: a stone fountain, water rising in silence, and a figure seated on the wide rim with their back to you. The air is different. Dry. The kind of dry that has been here a long time.

You have a body. It is whole. No injury. No sand in a knee. The road to Lagota is already in your mind like a line drawn on a map you have not yet unrolled.

The figure has not turned. They know you are here the way the stone knows what it holds.

The sitter is stone along the outside of both arms, stone in the knuckles, stone along the spine that catches the light differently than bone. A body built for weight. A body built to carry. The face is calm and the eyes are the colour of something older than the desert — not quite sand, not quite the blue you came from. Something in between.

They turn. The movement is unhurried, the way someone turns when they have already decided not to be hurried by the conversation.

"You know the way," they say. The voice is quiet, unhurried, the voice of someone who has learned patience from stone. "Lagota is east. The desert does not stop you from going east."

They wait. The sun has not moved. It will not move.

"What did you come carrying?"

## Turn 2

**Player:** Twelve blue stones. Transport tags. Someone wrote *Not them* on one of them before they were taken off the schedule. That's what I'm carrying — and the road east, which I don't need to be asked twice about.

**Game Master:**

The sitter's eyes track the stones in your hand without moving their head. The blue in them catches the light the way the conduit catches it — warm, artificial, the blue of empire stamps and empire seals.

"Not them," they repeat. The voice is quiet, unsurprised. "Someone removed a name from a list. The stones don't care. They go where they're told."

They stand. The movement is unhurried and the stone in their arms shifts with it, settles, resettles. They are not blocking the way. They are simply taller now.

"The road out east isn't the road in from the desert," they say. "It was closed after the harvest. Don't know who closed it. Don't know what they harvested. The town is unfinished at that edge — you walk out and the desert takes you again."

They look at the stones in your hand.

"Twelve stones. Twelve names on a list that someone decided not to take. That's not nothing. That's twelve people who are somewhere else right now because someone wrote two words."

The fountain rises behind them, water finding water, IAM NOTUM SUM facing the desert you came from.

"The road is east," they say. "But you should know what it leads."

You walk. The cobblestones give way to harder ground past the last building — a general store with boards in its windows. The commercial strip thins. The crooked steeple of the church leans east above you as you pass, the way it has been leaning since before you arrived.

The town's edge is not marked. One moment the ground is packed and holds your weight; the next it is sand and the sun is directly overhead and has not moved. The desert again. The desert is always there when you stop paying attention.

East is open. The horizon is flat and white. Somewhere past the white is Lagota. Somewhere past the white is whatever closed the road.

## Turn 3

**Player:** I walk into the white. The stones are warm in my pocket and the sun is where it has always been and the sand takes my boots the way sand does — not hindering, not helping, just present. The sitter said the road was closed after the harvest. Closed by whom, and what was harvested, is not a question that stops feet. I'll find out when I get there. Lagota is east. The stones are with me. That's enough to start.

**Game Master:**

You walk.

The white takes you in stages. First the ground loses its sense of path — packed earth giving way to something the desert decides rather than agrees to. Then the sounds go. No wind. No bird. The kind of silence that only happens when the world has stopped pretending to care what you do next.

Your boots sink exactly as far as they want to. The sun has not moved. The stones against your hip are warm — warmer than the sand, warmer than they should be. You press your hand to the pocket without stopping and feel the shape of them through the cloth, twelve small edges, one of them rough where the ink dried.

Something glints ahead. Then nothing. Then glints again, the way heat does when it is lying about what it sees.

You walk toward it.

The heat makes it hard to read at first. A frame. Tall. Metal — the blue-grey of empire infrastructure, the same alloy as the conduit housings you passed in the town square. But the blue is gone from this one. The frame stands open and empty, its aperture dark, its base cracked down the front like a jaw that bit something too hard. The blue tubes that should run from base to apex have been torn out at angles, some still sparking faintly where the connection died. The harvest left its tools behind: a frame, a wound, and the smell of something electrical that the desert is trying to forget.

There is no clock in the frame. There should be a clock. The one in the town square counted in blue — but this one was stopped mid-count, the hands fused where the harvest reached in and took whatever the clock was counting toward.

You are standing in front of a harvested transport gate. One of the roads the empire uses to move people — or to stop moving them. Whatever closed the eastern road did it by closing this. Taking the clock. Taking the blue. Leaving the frame as a message or a warning or simply a wound.

Your hand finds the pocket again. Twelve stones. Twelve names on a list that someone decided not to move. You think about what a harvested gate looks like — empty, stopped, unable to carry anyone — and you look at the stones in your hand.

The road to Lagota runs through this frame. The question is whether whatever harvested it is still close enough to stop you.

What do you do?
