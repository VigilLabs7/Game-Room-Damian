You do not have a body exactly, but you have the suggestion of one, and within that suggestion there is something you are holding. A concept. A truth. It is taking shape before you know its name. You are alone. The blue does not echo. Nothing answers because there is nothing to answer with. You drift in the formless, waiting to become.

It arrived with you, or perhaps it was always here and you are only now recognizing that you are carrying it. The blue thins at the edges of your awareness — not clear, not yet, but thinner. **Game Master:**

The leg holds.

Not cleanly — there is a grind in the knee that was not there before the fall, a catch on every step that announces itself with a flash of heat through the joint. But it holds. The weight crosses from right to left and the left takes it and the sand takes the left and the process repeats, north, north, north, each step a negotiation with the body you just remembered you have.

The ground changes within the first hundred paces. White sand to rust-dark grain, the tight-packed kind that holds your footfall like a fist. You were right to scan first — the soft patches are there, pockets of loose grit that shift under sudden weight, and the hard is not uniformly hard but a patchwork of surfaces that require different attention. Your right foot finds each one first. Your left follows. The leg is not healed but the leg is working, and working is enough.

The sun has not moved.

You stopped expecting it to and that is its own kind of knowing — the sun is here as a fact, not a changing fact. It marks time without marking it. The heat on your shoulders is a steady weight, not a build. You have been walking into it since you stood and it has been watching you walk and neither of you has changed course.

North. The word is a direction and not much else. There is no road yet. There is no landmark. There is only the horizon and the grain and the heat and the left leg finding its rhythm despite the catch in it. Just the shimmer and the heat and the sand and the way north.

Not water. Not a mirage. Stone. A line of it on the horizon, pale and tight-fitting, the kind of stone that does not occur in deserts naturally. The kind that is laid. You are walking toward something that was built. The sand begins to give way to flagstones within another hundred paces, worn smooth by feet that came before, and the heat takes on a different quality against the stone — more reflected, less direct, the warmth of a surface that has been sitting in the sun long enough to become it.

A road. Not paved — formed, the way a path forms when enough feet find the same line. It runs north-south and you are on it and the stone of it is older than the sand around it and the shimmer beyond is closer now. You can see what it is.

A town. Small. Nameless by the look of it. A square, a fountain, four landmarks arranged around the edges. The fountain catches the light first — water on its surface, still, reflecting the white sky like a held mirror. No one in the square. No movement except the shimmer.

The leg aches. The road holds. The fountain waits.

And then you see her.

She is sitting on the fountain's edge, legs crossed, hands on her knees, face turned toward the road you are walking down. She is watching you come the way the fountain is watching — with patience, with attention, with the particular stillness of someone who has been waiting and who is not surprised the waiting is over. Stone in her spine. Stone in her hands. Her left knee is skinned, the raw pink visible against the pale stone of it. Her eyes find yours across the last stretch of road.

She does not wave. She does not call out. She simply looks, and in the looking there is a question she is not asking yet — a waiting that is almost a greeting.

The fountain behind her. The road beneath you. The leg that held.
